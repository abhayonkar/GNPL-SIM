Academic Case Study · Open Data
Optimization of city gas network: a case study from Gujarat, India
Akbari D., Mehta M. — Discover Applied Sciences (Springer Nature, 2019)
v2 Gandhinagar Topology Pipe geometry
What was used: The 10-DRS + 10-CNG topology layout for Gandhinagar GA (Sabarmati Gas Limited service area). Hardy Cross nodal analysis methodology for steady-state validation. Pipe diameter and length ratios for DN250→DN80 spur sizing. The study area covers ~649 km² Gandhinagar district, confirming our ring-radial topology choice. Figure 1 of the paper shows the actual CGD study area map used for v2 node placement.

v3 topology ref Multi-CGS design
What was used: MGL's 5 city gate station radial pattern (Dharavi, Mahim, Andheri, Mulund, Vashi) confirmed that multi-CGS networks use ring-radial rather than single-source tree topologies. Petroleum & Natural Gas Regulatory Board (PNGRB), India — Consolidated 2016, Amended Dec 2024
v1 existing v2 v3 All parameters
What was used: MAOP 26 barg for Gujarat CGD networks (v2). IS 3589 / API 5L Gr.B pipe specifications (wall thickness vs DN). CGS equipment requirements: dual active/monitor regulators, slam-shut valve, pre-heater. DRS design: pilot-operated regulator, SSV, ESD at ±10% setpoint. MDPE PE-100 for secondary distribution. Gas quality: GCV ≥ 8,000 kcal/SCM. These parameters are implemented in the existing simConfig.m and extended into v2/v3 INFO panels.

── Implementation Notes
On v2 (Gandhinagar) pressure choice: Gujarat CGD networks operate at 26 barg MAOP (T4S standard). The existing simConfig.m uses 22 barg nominal source pressure with 26 barg MAOP, which is compatible with v2. No parameter changes needed to simulate v2 topology in MATLAB — only the incidence matrix, pipe_L, and pipe_D arrays would change.

On v3 (IGL Delhi) pressure: IGL's Delhi steel network operates at a lower nominal ring pressure (~19 barg) compared to Gujarat networks, due to the older IS 3589 pipe infrastructure and more conservative operating margins used by Delhi's urban network. If implementing v3 in the simulator, src_p_barg should be reduced from [22, 21] to [19, 18] and prs setpoints lowered accordingly. The DRS outlet of 4 barg (instead of v1's 14–18 barg) reflects IGL's two-stage reduction to domestic medium pressure.

On CNG offtake nodes: CNG stations connect directly to the HP steel ring (not through DRS) because they require high inlet pressure for cascade compression to 250 barg. In the simulator, CNG nodes can be modelled as demand nodes with a higher constant-pressure boundary condition (the CNG compressor maintains inlet pressure ~15 barg minimum).

On data provenance: Node coordinates and pipe lengths in v2 and v3 are estimated from publicly available city maps, PNGRB GA boundary documents, and the Springer case study figures. They are representative of the real networks, not exact surveyed values. The topological structure (ring vs radial, number of DRS zones) closely matches published operator data.
